What Restaurant Owners Should Know Before Introducing Table Ordering

Table ordering can unlock immense efficiency during peak hours, but without operational alignment, it can overwhelm your kitchen and confuse your floor team.

When implemented correctly, table ordering is a force multiplier. Guests in an expansive pub beer garden or bustling bistro can order another round of drinks or a dessert without craning their necks to catch a waiter's eye.

However, introducing digital ordering is fundamentally an operational change, not just a software installation. Here are the four critical realities every operator must evaluate before launch.

1. Kitchen & Bar Throughput Capacity

Traditional floor service creates a natural buffer: waiters take orders in rounds, stagger their inputs into the till, and give kitchen stations time to catch up. When tables can submit orders directly at any second, orders can arrive in sudden unthrottled clusters.

Before activating table ordering for food, verify that your kitchen display system (KDS) or printer setup supports order throttling and clear station routing (e.g. drinks to dispense bar, starters to cold line).

2. The Myth of Universal POS Compatibility

Be wary of any technology provider claiming their solution "plugs into every POS effortlessly". Restaurant EPOS environments in the UK are notoriously fragmented. Direct two-way API integrations require matching menu IDs, modifier trees, and tax settings.

If your existing POS lacks modern cloud APIs, a parallel tablet terminal or dedicated cloud ticket printer on the dispense bar often provides a much more stable, crash-free operational workflow.

3. Staff Training & Service Role Redefinition

When customers handle order entry themselves, what do your waiting staff do? They do not become redundant; their role shifts from transactional order-takers to attentive hospitality ambassadors. Floor staff must be trained to actively welcome diners, explain the table markers, and deliver drinks promptly.
